Sky behind the arena

Mockup only (nothing in the game yet). The big picture is a 1280x720 game frame, 1:1: an arena with the H walls, two ships drifting about, and the sky behind it. The sky moves opposite to the ships' average position, as Starfield does today, with each layer at its own depth (near layers move more). Switch the layers on and off below. Constellations are real star positions (bright stars only, RA and declination, rounded) drawn as faint line art; their layout on screen is by hand, not for any time or place. Planets are only dots in this mockup.

The four constellations on their own

Notes

  • Constellation lines are drawn very dim (about 25% white) with the stars on them a little brighter and bigger, so they read as a background, not as a map, and never compete with ships, shots and walls.
  • Three depths: far (small, moves 4% of the camera), middle (12%), near (25%). A constellation sits on one depth, so Orion drifts slowly and the Plough a little faster.
  • Stars on the lines twinkle by a few percent of brightness, slowly and out of step.
  • Planets: a dot (Saturn with a thin ring), a name label if labels are on, a slow drift of about a pixel a second.
  • The nebula is made of faint dots, like the stars (violet, magenta and teal, so it is not mistaken for the blue walls); "Nebula glow" is a soft painted version of the same cloud, to compare. The comet crosses every minute or so, with a short fading tail.
  • Real star positions: fixed in the game as a small table (names, RA, Dec, brightness). A real sky for the player's time and place would use a catalogue of a few hundred stars and is a separate decision.
